## Scores, dependence, and public grading

- **Sourced theory.** [Gneiting and Raftery (2007)](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf) supports strict propriety of probability/distribution scores, CRPS for a continuous margin, and interval scores that account for width and misses. [Gneiting, Balabdaoui and Raftery (2007)](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jrssb.pdf) defines calibration as consistency of forecasts with outcomes and sharpness as concentration, with sharpness sought subject to calibration. Proper scores reward honest distributions **in expectation**; one realized score does not establish calibration, uniquely identify a mechanism, or ensure every broad forecast loses to a sharper one. Coverage plus width is useful diagnostic context, not a substitute for a proper score. The public-communication memo cites the correct JASA PDF; the predictive-evaluation memo’s link labeled “Gneiting & Raftery (2007)” points to the **JRSSB paper by three authors** and should be relabeled or replaced with the JASA PDF. The predictive-evaluation memo’s “Murphy & Daan, scoring rules” DOI [10.1175/2009MWR2945.1](https://doi.org/10.1175/2009MWR2945.1) resolves in Crossref to **Benedetti (2010), “Scoring Rules for Forecast Verification”**; correct author/title before reuse.
- **Unsupported option for unordered outcomes.** The predictive-evaluation memo offers “multiclass Brier **or ranked probability score**” for a multi-candidate House winner. Ranked probability score accumulates errors across an **ordered** category scale; the [original RPS comparison](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/mwre/98/12/1520-0493_1970_098_0917_trpsat_2_3_co_2.xml) recommends it where the variable is ordered. Candidate/party names have no natural order. Use a full-vector multiclass Brier or categorical log score for a specified, exhaustive outcome space; RPS applies only if owners define a meaningful ordered target such as margin bands. A binary 50/50 reference is likewise limited to a genuinely binary outcome.
- **Sourced dependence warning; proposed diagnostic.** [Scheuerer and Hamill (2015)](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/22327/) says ensemble energy score has limited sensitivity to incorrectly specified correlations and develops a variogram score. [Grimmer, Knox and Westwood (2024 preprint)](https://dcknox.github.io/files/GrimmerKnoxWestwood_AssessingReliabilityPresidentialElectionForecasts.pdf) argues rare presidential cycles and within-year correlation sharply limit reliability assessment. This supports avoiding naive race-count confidence intervals. Which joint draws, weights, and variogram pairs would diagnose **this** House/Senate forecast is **proposed and unverified**. Do not imply a single chamber realization is a strong dependence test or that any proper score makes A–F cutoffs objective.
- **Public grade remains ungrounded.** No primary source here validates universal A–F thresholds, weights, expected-error tolerances, or a simple baseline that is feasible at the same 2026 information cutoff. Historical hindcasts, frozen contest cohorts, complete result definitions, and owner-selected targets are necessary; available hindcasts may still be too sparse or mismatched for defensible letters. Publish raw predicted/observed margins, signed/absolute errors, intervals, scores, baseline values, and unresolved status even if no grade can be defended. “Insufficient evidence” is an honest outcome. This is a research/owner gate, not a reason to suppress an informative watcher’s guide.

## Communication evidence and implementation boundary

The 50% win-chance versus margin distinction is mathematical and well supported; displaying expected margin, interval, and chance of a predeclared close band is a **proposal** whose exact bands and graphic require testing. [Westwood/Lelkes/Messing’s study summarized by its coauthor at Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2018/02/06/use-of-election-forecasts-in-campaign-coverage-can-confuse-voters-and-may-lower-turnout/) found probability framing increased perceived certainty versus vote-share framing; its turnout effect came from an **online voting game**, not observed 2016 turnout. The [Met Office Weather Game](https://gc.copernicus.org/articles/2/101/2019/) had 8,220 unique completers and task-dependent display effects; [Leffrang and Müller (2025)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/for.3222) used N=239 in time-series advice. These support testing comprehension, not declaring one election visual superior. Check accessible source retrieval before repeating finer claims from the Wiley-hosted rain-probability study, which timed out in this audit.

**Before implementation:** freeze the exact model/version/time and immutable forecast artifact; target denominators and winner categories (including conditional pairs, Other, runoffs); pre-outcome illustrative state/race and competitive-cohort rules; final-result source/status; feasible same-horizon baselines; selected proper scores and any joint-draw diagnostic. Correct the citations and 2016 labels above. Keep A–F withheld until an owner-reviewed, historically justified rubric is possible. No 2026 results or grades were inferred in this review.
