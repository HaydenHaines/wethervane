## Primary data contract and release timing

Every input row must bind agency, native series/geography, units, seasonal status, reference interval, public release timestamp, vintage/revision timestamp, URL, raw-byte SHA-256, transformation and cutoff. Retrieval time today does not establish historical availability. Select the latest eligible release at the declared cutoff, then compute all lags from that same release vintage. Preserve missingness and reasons. Do not silently use current revised API values, backfill from a future release, or infer state prices from unavailable observations.

### Gasoline

Start with EIA national **regular, all formulations, retail dollars per gallon**, series `EMM_EPMR_PTE_NUS_DPG`, weekly. Do not substitute all grades or wholesale crude oil. The [official historical table](https://www.eia.gov/dnav/pet/hist/LeafHandler.ashx?f=W&n=PET&s=EMM_EPMR_PTE_NUS_DPG) supplies reference dates. The [EIA methodology](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/gasdiesel/gas_proc-methods.php) describes Monday outlet observations and a May 14, 2018 gasoline sample/method change, with explicit noncomparability to the preceding sample. The [release schedule](https://www.eia.gov/reports/upcoming.php) lists the gasoline/diesel update for Monday by 5 p.m.; historical holidays and actual archived publication must be checked, rather than assuming reference date equals availability. Do not confuse this with Wednesday petroleum inventories. The [2022 diesel change FAQ](https://www.eia.gov/petroleum/gasdiesel/dieselfuel_faqs.php) explicitly says that change did not update gasoline methodology.

Use a four-released-week trailing mean of log price and a predeclared 13-week change as one salience feature; do not search many windows on the four replay cycles. For 2018 the 13-week window is after the gasoline methodology break; a year-over-year change crosses it and needs an explicit sensitivity/bridge, not an invisible splice. Current historical tables establish numerical history, not original release bytes. Acquire dated archives or retain a retrospective-vintage qualification. No state-complete weekly gasoline panel is established here: EIA publishes selected states/cities and regions, not an automatically complete state panel.

### Headline and underlying inflation

Use BLS CPI-U U.S. city average NSA all-items `CUUR0000SA0` and all-items-less-food-and-energy `CUUR0000SA0L1E`; calculate twelve-month changes from release tables. Core CPI is an operational underlying-price proxy, not a causal or perfect latent inflation estimate. Gasoline is already inside headline CPI; headline-minus-core is a contrast, not an exact additive gasoline contribution. Do not assign a national CPI value the status of measured state inflation. [BLS concepts](https://www.bls.gov/opub/hom/cpi/concepts.htm) also warns that index levels across areas are not comparable price levels.

NSA year-over-year changes reduce the seasonal-vintage problem, but still preserve original release bytes and corrections. [BLS seasonal-adjustment documentation](https://www.bls.gov/cpi/seasonal-adjustment/) says seasonally adjusted histories can change for five years; current SA downloads would contaminate replay. Faster three-month annualized core inflation is a later, separately versioned alternative needing archived seasonal factors, not a free extra feature.

### Purchasing power and exposure

Use BEA national real disposable personal income **per person**, not nominal income or aggregate real GDP, with a predeclared twelve-month log change. Preserve the official real-income/population vintage and chain-dollar basis; use ratios within a vintage rather than combining differently based levels. Real DPI includes taxes/transfers and is not a household median, wage-only or identical to voters' experienced budgets. The [September 2020 release](https://www.bea.gov/news/2020/personal-income-and-outlays-september-2020) discusses large pandemic-program effects; those motivate a sensitivity, not post hoc removal of 2020. If the available release lacks the required per-capita lag, acquire its full vintage table or leave the feature unavailable.

For a later state interaction, use a **predetermined** gasoline burden proxy from an admitted pre-election annual edition, centered across states using fixed training-only weights. [EIA SEDS](https://www.eia.gov/state/seds/seds-data-complete.php) offers state energy consumption/prices/expenditures but is lagged: the current complete 1960–2024 edition was released in June 2026. A 2024 reference year therefore cannot simply be assigned to a 2024 cutoff. SEDS motor-gasoline expenditures are not automatically household out-of-pocket spending; document sector coverage and avoid calling them voter budgets. ACS commuting/vehicle availability could provide a separate predetermined proxy, but its survey universe/edition and existing type/geography overlap need review. None is ready to join without dated original editions.

## At most three nested challengers

Let F0 be the exact currently selected joint fundamentals law. Each challenger changes only the fundamentals mean; the unchanged environment residual/covariance, participation/choice, poll likelihood, source ownership and original parent weights remain part of the coherent refitted graph. Do not add these as a second post-poll shift. Code economic direction relative to the source-admitted presidential party and transform once to the model's choice coordinate; state incumbency and presidential-party membership are different variables.

1. **F1 = F0 + national inflation block.** Add standardized core twelve-month inflation and headline-minus-core contrast. This spans headline/core information without three nearly collinear raw series. Retain the existing midterm intercept; optionally permit one strongly pooled midterm interaction on the block only after adequate earlier cycles exist. The headline-only restriction is the internal low-risk comparator. Do not assume core necessarily predicts better than headline. Evidence of an independent feature requires incremental out-of-cycle predictive value beyond F0's national residual, not a significant regression fitted to thousands of duplicated race rows.
2. **F2 = F1 + gasoline salience increment.** Add the one predeclared weekly gasoline-change feature, residualized against F1 covariates using training data only, with its projection retained for prediction. This asks whether gasoline's timely/salient information adds beyond monthly inflation. Include both national common-effect and midterm-specific coefficient through strong hierarchical pooling only if training support permits; the low-risk first fit is one common coefficient. A sign reversal is admissible. Cheap gasoline during a demand collapse is the obvious confounder; no causal penalty or “bad economy” interpretation follows automatically. Compare against the F2 zero-coefficient restriction, not another independently drawn gas shock.
3. **F3 = F2 + real-purchasing-power block.** Add one real-DPI-per-capita growth feature. A state gasoline-exposure interaction is a gated secondary restriction of this block, not an assumed observed state-price effect: common national gas change times centered pre-cutoff exposure, separate from national level. Omit it entirely until dated exposure coverage exists. Test the income-only restriction first; combining several state-income/wage/industry/vehicle proxies immediately would duplicate implemented economic/type mechanisms and overfit. Real-income growth already deflates prices; the inflation coefficients here describe incremental information/possible salience, not an accounting decomposition or separate causal cost.

Use training-only standardization and skeptical symmetric Gaussian coefficient priors; an initial proposal is SD 0.05 in logit choice per training SD of a national feature, and SD 0.025 for a midterm/exposure interaction. These are proposed constraints requiring prior-predictive checks in the owner's graph, not inherited qualified priors or estimates. Hierarchical scales must be fixed or learned inside earlier-cycle training, not chosen from 2018/2022 losses. Keep unchanged F0 priors where possible; recompute every posterior jointly. Do not introduce a forced negative sign, inflation threshold selected on 2022, unconstrained state slopes, or a separate latent economic shock sharing the same evidence twice.

## Verification, scarce national variation and interpretation

A national macro feature has election-cycle sample size, not district/race sample size. Four A/B cycles are evaluation comparisons, not sufficient independent training observations for this block. A consistent national gasoline series starts later than the legacy 1974 midterm table, so specify a common historical start (at least 1992 for a possible EIA-based even-year panel, subject to actual archived coverage). Do not pretend its pre-1990 missing values were observed. Earlier presidential and midterm cycles can share strongly pooled effects with a midterm indicator; a midterm-only series leaves very few cycles and may justify only the headline-only restriction. An admitted older outcome/roster/map/macro panel is a prerequisite, not established by this memo. If the current owner panel begins in 2010, the 2018 replay has only four earlier even-year cycles: report the limitation and obtain earlier admissible data before claiming identified multi-feature macro effects.

For each A2018/A2022 and B2020/B2024 target, train all fitted labels on strictly earlier elections, reproduce that target's H14 release joins, and keep the same contests, observation masks, rosters, target estimand and admitted parent support across arms. No revised target-year outcomes may tune representation, windows, priors or state exposure. Use the project's iterative historical replay workflow; these repeatedly visited years are development evidence, not untouched prospective tests. The 2026 forecast eventually uses the chosen specification trained through admitted earlier outcomes and actual 2026-cutoff covariates, without 2026 outcome labels.

Evaluate F0→F1→F2→F3 one addition at a time, with matched full-graph refits and numerical/Monte Carlo precision gates. First run fundamentals-only arms to assess new information independently of polls; then use the **same existing polling architecture and source inputs** in paired full-stack arms to test incremental value after polling. A gain disappearing after polls is useful negative evidence, not license to double-weight the macro term. Root/House owner controls existing polling experiments; this memo starts none.

Predeclare primary proper scores for the owner's actual target (group/candidate final winner log score/Brier, or share CRPS when that is the declared aggregate comparator), calibration/concentration diagnostics and practical regression guards. Retain cycle-level scores, equal-cycle A/B summaries and competitive-race subsets without treating their duplicated national covariate as independent evidence. Do not infer control performance from race scores or transfer historical aggregate House-share qualification to Senate candidate counts. No universal every-cycle/every-metric improvement requirement: reject unnecessary added complexity when incremental gains are not practically distinguishable, when material guarded regressions occur, or when gains vanish under valid vintage/admission corrections. Retaining F0 or an earlier nested restriction is a successful decision.

The first low-risk next step is the vintage-aware **headline-only F1 restriction**, alongside its core/contrast extension as a prespecified later comparison. Gasoline is the clearest explicit source-adaptation gap, but inflation and purchasing power are already partially represented in legacy models and shared residuals. None is presently established as the weakest layer, a causal mechanism, or an empirically superior forecasting model.
