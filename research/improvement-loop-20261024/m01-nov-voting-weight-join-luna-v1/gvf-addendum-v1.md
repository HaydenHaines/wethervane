# Addendum: official generalized variance functions qualify replicate-file requirement

This is a separate addendum to `README.md`; it preserves the original directory-listing finding and clarifies its scope. Primary-source check retrieved 2026-10-26. No microdata or replicate data were acquired or evaluated.

## Correction to the practical implication

The previous report identified missing year-specific 2018/2020 November Voting replicate files as a gap to close a replicate-file join question. That does **not** mean such a file is strictly necessary for every uncertainty calculation. The 2018 and 2020 official technical documentation publishes year-specific generalized variance function (GVF) parameters for November Voting and Registration characteristics (Table 8), plus formulas for approximate standard errors of totals, percentages and differences. These supply an official alternative for a defined set of aggregate estimates without obtaining a public voting replicate file.

The two documents say the GVF parameters are estimated using direct replicate variances, then provide the fitted parameters as an easier way to obtain approximate standard errors for numerous characteristics. The published Table 8 is therefore evidence that replicate-based calculations underlie Census's GVFs; it is not a public record-level replicate-weight layout or an instruction for joining a replicate file to person rows.

For an estimated number `x`, formula (1) is `SE(x) = sqrt(a*x^2 + b*x)`. For a percentage `p=100*x/y`, formula (2) is `SE(p) = sqrt((b/y)*p*(100-p))`. These are approximate marginal errors using the designated `a,b` parameters. The docs give rules for using the parameter set associated with the characteristic and provide race/ethnicity group choices; for cross-tabulations involving different characteristics, they direct users to the parameters yielding the largest standard error.

The difference formula (3), `SE(x1-x2) ≈ sqrt(SE(x1)^2 + SE(x2)^2)`, is expressly described as accurate for estimates of the same characteristic in two different areas or for separate, uncorrelated characteristics in one area. Census warns that with high positive or negative correlation between characteristics it overestimates or underestimates the true standard error, respectively. This is not a joint covariance estimator.

## What this does and does not solve

- **Aggregate marginal uncertainty:** Yes, the published GVF parameters are an official, feasible alternative for approximate standard errors for covered November Voting/Registration characteristics and listed breakdowns. They can support Census-style uncertainty summaries when the target estimate matches the table's characteristic, population base, race/ethnicity class and geography rules.
- **Arbitrary microdata function or joint likelihood:** No. Tables provide parameterized marginal variances, not a person-level replicate matrix or a covariance matrix across all cells/characteristics. A hierarchical likelihood involving a vector of linked voting/registration outcomes, many poststrata, or correlated latent components cannot be reconstructed from separate marginal standard errors alone. More information—e.g. validated public replicate records with design procedure, a design covariance product, or an explicitly approximate working likelihood—is needed.
- **Subgroups:** Table 8 supports specified categories/breakdowns, and the docs describe parameters for race/ethnicity and geography. This is not a universal guarantee for arbitrary small domains or very fine intersections. The supplied formulas do not create information for unsupported cells, and their approximate standard error may be inadequate for a joint model. A fit using GVFs would need a predeclared mapping from every modeled cell to documented Table 8 parameters and population base; no such evaluation is performed here.
- **Registration skip:** In both years, the record layout gives `PES2` (“registered to vote”) the edited universe `PES1=2,-2,-3,-9`; this is a coding/universe definition, not a claim about which exact response paths led to an administered question. The questionnaire routing explicitly sends S1=No, Don't Know, or Refused to S2 (PES2), while S1=Yes goes to S5 and skips S2. The questionnaire does not show a separate No Response route to S2. The `-9` code appears in the edited-file universe/valid-entry definitions, but that alone does not establish that an interviewer asked S2 after a no-response case. People reporting Yes to voting are outside the PES2 universe. Thus the supplement does not observe registration status for all people who report voting; `PES1` and `PES2` are not complete jointly observed R/V outcomes for everyone. `PES7` has a broader universe (`PES1=1` or `PES2=1`) but asks method of the last registration, not registered/not-registered status. The GVF does not resolve this missingness/skip pattern or identify a full registration→voting transition without additional assumptions/data.
- **Historical provenance:** These year-labelled technical documents are authoritative public documentation of the published GVF procedures and estimates. The documents state that parameter estimation used direct replicate variances from CPS/internal files, but the public docs do not establish a public, year-specific November Voting replicate file, its release date, record-level lineage, or a reproducible person join. The technical-doc posting date is also not proof of original historical availability by a modeling cutoff.

## Bottom line

Year-specific replicate files are not strictly necessary to obtain official approximate *marginal standard errors* for covered CPS Voting/Registration aggregates: the 2018/2020 GVF tables and formulas are public. They do not supply the person-level replicate join, general design covariance, complete joint registration/voting measurement, or historical file-vintage provenance needed to claim a validated record-level joint likelihood. The original question “does a documented, validated 2018/2020 November Voting replicate-weight join exist?” remains unresolved; the broader claim “replicate weights are required for all uncertainty work” would be too strong.
