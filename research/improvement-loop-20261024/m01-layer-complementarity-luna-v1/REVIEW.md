# Source review: candidate allocation and participation evidence ownership

## Scope and verdict

This is a read-only synthesis of the frozen A2018 plan, its selected `117-components.py` snapshot, the existing candidate-calibration review, and the unadmitted synthetic role-aware and participation alternatives. I inspected source and existing aggregate receipts only: no posterior draws/raw rows, data mutation, fits, or jobs.

The source supports **complementary mechanisms that interact**, not an empirical ranking of which layer is weakest. The current 2018 plan selects `candidate_iid_profile_choice_v1` and `profile_turnout_response_observed_total_gamma_v1`; it does not select the available role-hierarchy provider. Its eight parent masses remain exactly 0.125 each. The plan itself is marked `national_complete=false`, `publication_authorized=false`, and `forecast_qualified=false`.

No matched joint refit or outcome comparison is present in these alternatives. The prototypes are synthetic probability/moment helpers, not fitted likelihoods or model extensions. The 2018 write-in discrepancy motivates a candidate-allocation hypothesis; it is not turnout evidence. A matched refit could test interaction, but needs a single coherent observation/response/count law and separate, properly timed outcome sources first.

## What existing outcome evidence does and does not establish

The candidate-calibration README and pinned diagnostic inventory describe Arizona 2018 as having 20 target major-pair poll rows but no target-event survey, official-count factor, or participation-report factor. The separate score review records 566 combined write-in marks against a predicted mean around 587,861. That is a severe candidate-mark allocation discrepancy under the reviewed posterior output, not a physical-turnout comparison. The candidate write-in utility summaries are close to the `Normal(-3,1)` prior, consistent with limited learning about those particular effects; they do not establish posterior equality to prior or causal attribution.

The major-pair poll likelihood aggregates over profiles, so a minor utility can affect it indirectly by changing profile-specific softmax denominators; participation intensity can affect it by changing profile weights. This structural interaction supports a joint test. It does not identify which mechanism caused the Arizona discrepancy. Because this target and the Nevada 2022 participation evidence have already been examined to formulate the alternatives, scoring those same targets after tuning these alternatives would be exposed retrospective development evidence, not prospective validation.

## Naive composition hazards

1. **Two candidate mass systems:** retaining the full all-candidate softmax while also imposing a role-level `other_mass` can apply two unrelated controls to the same candidate partition. Choose and define one normalized allocation law. If group masses and role utilities are both estimated, the model must give each a clear conditional role: first a learned role-level mass, then candidate probabilities within role.
2. **Response duplicated or confused with allocation:** `response` is a contest-level scalar separating named candidate marks from noncandidate marks. `other_mass` is conditional on a candidate mark and divides candidate choices. They are different quantities. Fitting a new response parameter inside the role prototype while retaining the native `response` duplicates that dimension. A cell-specific response would also be a different model from the pinned scalar-response source.
3. **Physical totals counted twice:** current category-count rates, reports, and an alternative finite-population count can all bear on a contest's event total. If ballot categories exhaust the same ballots counted by a report, treating them as conditionally independent totals needs a defensible measurement model. The synthetic participation helper omits the native eventG, response, report bias, and candidate-count factors, so it cannot settle this ownership question.
4. **Confounding from ratios:** ordinary pair polls identify candidate-rate ratios, not common event scale. Their scalar response/eventG factors cancel, while profile mixture effects remain. A poll-only target therefore cannot validate a population participation scale; it may still inform compositional profile weights.
5. **Outcome reuse:** candidate masses, role priors, participation transitions, and response assumptions must be trained only on pre-cutoff data. The previously inspected 2018/2022 target outcomes cannot be used both to choose this architecture and to present a confirmatory held-out score.
6. **Protected parent cut:** retain the original eight parent masses at 0.125. Normalize only each parent's conditional law where required by the selected cut; do not update outer masses from evidence as a supposed parity comparison.

## Concrete matched comparison that could test complementarity

First build one integrated, versioned candidate-count and participation law with an explicit observation ledger. The ledger should identify, for every official count/report/survey/poll factor, its unit, event, source, conditioning variables, and whether it observes ballots, participating people, intended candidate marks, or noncandidate categories. Establish cutoff-valid rosters/roles, integer or uncertain eligible-person support, and independent participation measurements before fitting. Do not treat exposure estimates as exact eligible person counts or CPS self-report as the same universe as administrative totals without a measurement bridge.

Then run an exploratory matched 2×2 factorial refit on the same predeclared training data and target set:

| Arm | Candidate allocation | Participation/count scale |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | Existing IID full softmax | Existing profile propensity plus eventG |
| 10 | Learned role-level group mass, then within-role candidate allocation | Existing participation/count scale |
| 01 | Existing candidate allocation | A single finite-population participation law replacing—not supplementing—the competing q/G path |
| 11 | The same role-level allocation | The same single participation replacement |

Use identical source cutoff, data, priors outside the targeted changes, sampler settings, seeds/replication plan, and fixed outer parent identities and 0.125 masses. Keep the response scalar shared across all arms unless the design explicitly adds a separately observed response mechanism. In every arm retain aligned posterior joint draws; marginal-mean products cannot quantify cell-profile covariance. Report outcomes separately: proper predictive performance/calibration for physical participation measures, complete candidate/noncandidate mark vectors, and the joint vector where one generative outcome law is defined. Include diagnostics and runtime, and compare interaction (arm 11 vs the two single changes) rather than infer “complementarity” from marginal component means.

The first use of the already inspected Arizona 2018 and Nevada 2022 labels can only be a clearly marked exploratory replay. For a clean confirmatory test, preregister this four-arm comparison on a genuinely unexposed later cycle or a cycle whose outcomes were not used to select the candidate architecture. Do not run until the source/measurement ledger and resource/qualification gates are separately satisfied.

## Bounded conclusion

The source establishes quantity ownership and plausible coupling, but supplies no new empirical estimate of layer-specific effect or improvement. Candidate and participation mechanisms are not orthogonal: both can influence profile-weighted category rates, while common scale and scalar response cancel in ordinary pair ratios. The prototypes are not jointly identifiable or directly composable as written. A matched, jointly refit factorial can test the hypothesis only after one coherent role-mass/response/participation observation model exists. This review makes no “weakest layer” claim and does not qualify a forecast.
