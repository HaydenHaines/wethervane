## Interpretation: whole forecasts, not an isolated layer ranking

FversusFP is a matched whole-forecast comparison of adding the reviewed polling update to a particular fundamentals forecast, conditional on its retained laws/data/banks. The direction table supports realized directional bias and narrowing tradeoffs:2018 polling moves the combined forecast toward results on these districts;2020combined errors reverse direction while intervals narrow. It does NOT allocate a unique causal fraction of error to fundamentals versus polling. The layers interact through posterior-bank reweighting/shared national effects, chosen uncertainty/calibration laws and source selection. A wide F interval can cover more while providing worse proper scores; a narrower FP interval can improve some scores and worsen others. Direction/coverage alone cannot select a calibrated layer or model.

PversusFP compares another complete forecast pair; better FPperformance can reflect regularization/coupling as implemented, not proof the fundamentals module is intrinsically superior. Existing benchmark README reports proper-score comparisons beyond this direction study, including2020Brier/CRPS disagreements. Those claims were not recomputed from raw draws here and remain separate from coverage. No marginal difference decomposition or joint matched-refit ablation was added.

Polled districts are selected(115/82/65/62), not a random sample of435. Race/outcome matching prevents one cohort mismatch, but not poll-selection bias, cross-district shared error, shared sample/calibration uncertainty or dependence among the4model variants. Only4elections are observed; they are not thousands of independent district trials. Same-direction misses are compatible with a shared shock, but this diagnostic does not estimate covariance or determine its source. It does not test the covariance/range/tails of a joint seat forecast. Do not construct independent-binomial coverage significance from these race counts.
